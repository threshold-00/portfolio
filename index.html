Discovery · Case study

SAF3 - A secure crypto custodian

NAB x Zodia Custody

The customer
Crypto native fund managers, ultra high-net-worth individuals
Project team
Laura Dineen (Product Manager), Adrian Ciaschetti (Crypto SME), Danielle Khalil (Risk/Business analyst), Kate Cooper (Executive), myself, engineering team.
Skills
  • Product design (end-to-end)
  • Crypto
  • MVP creation
  • Workshop facilitation
  • Mentoring non-designers/directors
  • Research via self-recruitment
  • Developer handover

Problem

Large institutional and ultra high-net-worth investors needed a secure and regulated way to store and manage digital assets. Existing custody solutions lacked the controls and trust required by this segment.

My scope

Defined first pass of MVP, refining features and design through research. Developed framework for our target customers. Designed entire platform, alongside assisting development efforts/QA. Assisted business development conversations.

Approach

  • Conducted and managed 10 interviews with customers who have an interest in crypto
  • Aligned with engineers on build possibilities and designed first pass at platform
  • Conducted another 10 customer interviews a mix of institutions (eg. JB Were) and crypto-native fund managers through targeted guerrilla recruitment.
  • Enabled the broader team to continue discreet customer research during business development engagements through hypothesis setting
  • Synthesised insights into product requirements and decision frameworks.

Outcome

Delivered an MVP aligned to customer needs, including a risk management feature and custody.

Overall findings included:

  • Leading with a, “custody” focus for comms was beneficial for our target customer (asset fund managers)
  • Risk management was a key feature to be implemented
  • Customer preferred broader coin range than just the main (BTC/ETH)

Reflection

On a personal note, I enjoyed the face-paced and at times, challenging nature of this work. It was rewarding to have such a large impact on this venture.

Research · Case study

Victorian Public Transport, Contactless Ticketing Pilot

Department of Transport

The customer
Victorian public transport commuters and station staff (gate attendants, station masters).
Project team
Adelaide Wood (Researcher), Mel Turkovic (Delivery Lead), Myself (Researcher)
Skills
  • Strategic design
  • Research ops
  • Ethnographic studies
  • Report writing
  • Working with Government

Problem

With contactless payment being introduced, the Department needed to ensure the new ticket reader system was intuitive, accessible, and acceptable to the public, and and that it would not create friction at gates or worsen station congestion.

My scope

Led research direction and synthesis. Worked with Department of Transport product and second user researcher to setup, conduct and develop research into an official report.

Approach

Triangulated three research methods to understand customer-machine interaction in a real-world setting:

  • Longitudinal Diary Study: 296 submissions over 8 weeks (2 pre-installation, 6 post-installation).
  • Ethnographic Observation: 42 hours of on-site observation, >6,700 individual tap-on/off interactions recorded.
  • Intercept Interviews: 112 customer and staff interviews at stations.

Insights focused on HMI (Human machine interface), audio, and the physical reader, alongside learnability and environmental factors (noise, crowding, positioning). We prioritised gathering data from those with accessibility needs.

Outcome

On average, customers rated tapping 20% easier after new readers were installed.

Designed recommendations to improve usability without slowing gate throughput, including:

  • Increasing visibility of secondary (balance) text without compromising privacy
  • Raising awareness of the “hold to extend display time” feature
  • Adjusting beep volume to (at the minimum) match station ambient noise levels (not exceeding +10db)

These findings directly informed final specification adjustments ahead of statewide rollout.

Reflection

We reached point of saturation with customer results around week 2 of ethnographic research, if I were to do this again, I would not advocate for more than 3-4 weeks of research.

Execution · Case study

Enhancing conversion for Afterpay’s B2B team

Dijitally x Afterpay US, UK (Clearpay), AU

The customer
Afterpay’s prospective business’
Project team
Optimisation Lead/UX (Myself), Growth Marketing Manager (Nick Petrillo), UI Designer (Dominque Vella)
Skills
  • Increase frequency

Problem and scope

Afterpay’s global team needed a program which allowed for continuous testing and improvements to their B2B facing site. The key part of this site being a form which business’ would fill out to start their application process with Afterpay.

Approach

  • Setup CRO playbook, working with client to define responsibilities and scope of design and testing
  • Created master tracker to manage testing and incorporate UX audit findings - received some push-back, but assured team that this program is about learnings and being open-minded.
  • Metrics were supplied by data scientists from Afterpay’s side and implemented into my recommendations
  • Designed wireframes, brief and dev notes for UI designer to finalise
  • Worked with delivery lead to manage testing timelines and client expectations

Outcome

Launched phase 1 of testing which included over 10 tests, a combination of new designs with multivariate tests.

Reflection

Whilst we were not able to conduct usability testing for this first round, I’ve been advocating for this - some of the metrics the client wanted to test (eg. scroll depth) are too subjective to test in A/B. I’m looking to incorporate this into the second phase of testing.
